*This is the sample bank one house's Paper draws from — a valley town in Northern California, which is why its local shelf is that valley. Seed your own the same way: local plants in their seasons, your town's past, and a year of on-this-days in art and science.*

*Every item below was fact-checked against the named source(s) on the date noted. The bank is the composer's shelf: editions draw from it first (docs/OPERATIONS.md Spec A has the rules — rotation across the three shelves, a 30-day no-repeat window tracked in a usage log the routine keeps, and the verification discipline for anything researched fresh). ## On This Day — art & science

### OTD-1101-WEGENER · Nov 1 · science
**Headline:** Born on this day: Alfred Wegener (1880)
**Text:** The German meteorologist and geophysicist Alfred Wegener was born on this day in 1880. In 1912 he proposed that the continents were once a single landmass — he called it Pangaea — and had slowly drifted apart; most geologists rejected the idea in his lifetime. Vindicated in the 1960s, continental drift became the foundation of plate tectonics.
**Sources:** Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Alfred Wegener" (britannica.com/biography/Alfred-Wegener); Wikipedia, "Alfred Wegener". Verified Nov 1, 2026.

### OTD-1101-CHASE · Nov 1 · art
**Headline:** Born on this day: William Merritt Chase (1849)
**Text:** The American painter William Merritt Chase was born on this day in 1849, in Williamsburg, Indiana (now Nineveh). A leading American Impressionist and perhaps the most influential art teacher of his generation, in 1896 he founded the Chase School in New York — the school that later became Parsons School of Design. Among his students at the Art Students League was a young Georgia O'Keeffe.
**Sources:** Wikipedia, "William Merritt Chase"; Sullivan Goss Art Gallery, artist chronology. Verified Nov 1, 2026.

### OTD-1107-CURIE · Nov 7 · science
**Headline:** Born on this day: Marie Curie (1867)
**Text:** Marie Skłodowska–Curie was born on this day in 1867, in Warsaw. She shared the 1903 Nobel Prize in Physics for research on radioactivity, then won the 1911 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for discovering the elements radium and polonium. She remains the only person ever to win Nobel Prizes in two different sciences.
**Sources:** NobelPrize.org, "Marie Curie — Facts" (nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/1903/marie-curie/facts/); Wikipedia, "Marie Curie". Verified Nov 1, 2026.

### OTD-1115-OKEEFFE · Nov 15 · art
**Headline:** Born on this day: Georgia O'Keeffe (1887)
**Text:** Georgia O'Keeffe was born on this day in 1887, on a dairy farm near Sun Prairie, Wisconsin. Called the mother of American modernism, she painted for seven decades — the magnified flowers and the New Mexico landscapes are hers — and in 1946 the Museum of Modern Art gave her a retrospective, its first for a woman artist.
**Sources:** Georgia O'Keeffe Museum, "About Georgia O'Keeffe" (okeeffemuseum.org); Wikipedia, "Georgia O'Keeffe". Verified Nov 1, 2026.

## The Valley — native plants & creatures of the Morgan Hill / Santa Clara Valley area

### VAL-NEWT · season: November–March (opens with the first rains)
**Headline:** The Valley: the newts are on the move
**Text:** With the first rains, California newts leave the hillsides and walk — slowly, overland — to the ponds and slow streams where they breed; their season runs roughly November to March. Their skin carries tetrodotoxin, the same poison as pufferfish, so the rule is look, never touch. After rain, watch for them on shaded trails near Coyote Creek; East Bay parks close South Park Drive to cars from November 1 to March 31 so the newts can cross.
**Sources:** East Bay Regional Park District, "The Newts of Tilden" (ebparks.org); National Park Service, California newt (nps.gov). Verified Nov 1, 2026.

### VAL-TOYON · season: October–December (berries), winter interest
**Headline:** The Valley: toyon turns red
**Text:** Toyon is the evergreen shrub turning red along the foothill trails right now. Its pomes ripen from October into December and hang on into winter — a standing feast for cedar waxwings, robins, and coyotes. It is also called Christmas berry, or California holly; look for it on the dry slopes of El Toro and throughout Henry Coe.
**Sources:** Wikipedia, "Heteromeles arbutifolia"; Plant Native, "Toyon" (plantnative.org). Verified Nov 1, 2026.

### VAL-VALLEYOAK · season: October–November (acorn fall, leaf turn)
**Headline:** The Valley: the valley oak's harvest
**Text:** The valley oak is the largest oak in North America — old trees pass 100 feet, spread wider than they are tall, and can live 600 years. Its acorns fall in October, and acorn woodpeckers, scrub-jays, and ground squirrels race to cache them; by November the harvest is stored and the leaves turn yellow, then brown. The giants along the Coyote Creek corridor are this tree.
**Sources:** Calscape / California Native Plant Society, "Valley Oak" (calscape.org); Wikipedia, "Quercus lobata". Verified Nov 1, 2026.

### VAL-ANNA · season: year-round; courtship from December
**Headline:** The Valley: the hummingbird that stays
**Text:** The hummingbird at the feeder in November is almost certainly an Anna's — the one West Coast hummingbird that stays all winter instead of migrating. The male's crown and throat flash iridescent rose, and nesting can begin as early as December, when his courtship dive ends in a loud pop made not by his voice but by his tail feathers.
**Sources:** Audubon Field Guide, "Anna's Hummingbird" (audubon.org); National Park Service, "Anna's Hummingbird" (nps.gov). Verified Nov 1, 2026.

## Local History — the valley's past

### HIS-VILLA · any date (strongest near Nov 10, the incorporation anniversary)
**Headline:** From the valley's past: the house that named a town
**Text:** Morgan Hill is named for Hiram Morgan Hill, a Missouri cattleman who built his country home here in the mid-1880s — Villa Mira Monte, on Monterey Road, construction begun in 1884 and finished in 1886. The Southern Pacific depot up the road was called Huntington Station, but passengers kept asking for "Morgan Hill's place," and the railroad gave in: the station, then the town, took his name. The town incorporated on November 10, 1906; the house is now the Morgan Hill Historical Society's home.
**Sources:** Morgan Hill Historical Society, "Villa Mira Monte — Construction and Architecture" (morganhillhistoricalsociety.org); City of Morgan Hill, Historic Context Statement (2006); Wikipedia, "Villa Mira Monte". Verified Nov 1, 2026.

### HIS-DELIGHT · any date (strongest in spring blossom season)
**Headline:** From the valley's past: the Valley of Heart's Delight
**Text:** Before it was Silicon Valley, this was the Valley of Heart's Delight. For a century the Santa Clara Valley held more than 100,000 acres of orchards and stood as the largest fruit-producing and packing region in the world. The nickname came from the spring: prunes, apricots, cherries, and pears in blossom at once, until the valley floor looked snow-covered. The orchard era lasted into the 1960s.
**Sources:** California Prunes, "The Valley of Heart's Delight" (californiaprunes.org). Verified Nov 1, 2026.

### HIS-ANDERSON · any date
**Headline:** From the valley's past: Anderson Lake
**Text:** Anderson Lake, east of town on Coyote Creek, was created in 1950, when Anderson Dam closed across the creek. The dam and lake are named for Leroy Anderson, a key founder and the first president of the Santa Clara Valley Water District. It is the county's largest reservoir — full, it holds more water than the district's other nine reservoirs combined.
**Sources:** Wikipedia, "Anderson Lake (California)". Verified Nov 1, 2026.

### HIS-COE · any date
**Headline:** From the valley's past: the park at the back door
**Text:** Henry W. Coe State Park, the wilderness at Morgan Hill's back door, began as Pine Ridge Ranch, run by rancher Henry W. Coe. In 1953 his daughter, Sada Coe Robinson, donated the 12,230-acre ranch to become a park in his memory. It has since grown past 87,000 acres — the largest state park in Northern California.
**Sources:** Henry W. Coe State Park guide (sfbaywalk.com, transcribing park materials); Wikipedia, "Henry W. Coe State Park". Verified Nov 1, 2026.
